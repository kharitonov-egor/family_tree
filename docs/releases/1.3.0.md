# Family Tree 1.3.0

## Chest menus for players without the client mod

Players who join a Family Tree server with an unmodded client can now type `/familytree` to browse in chest menus. Server admins no longer need every player to install the client mod.

- The pet list shows spawn egg icons, names, age, and living or deceased status, 45 pets to a page. Filter by status or species, or search by name in an anvil.
- Click a pet to see its parents, grandparents, and children, and click any relative to move through the family.
- Owners and operators can locate the pet in chat, rename it in Family Tree through an anvil, add or clear its parents with a picker and a confirmation step, and read the family as a written book on a lectern screen.
- The menus follow the screens' permissions and cooldowns. They never show coordinates, and other players' pets are view-only.
- Every click in a menu is refused before Minecraft moves an item. Shift-click, number keys, dragging, double-click, the offhand key, dropping, and closing with an item on the cursor leave the player's inventory unchanged.
- Players with the client mod keep the full screens. `/familytree` prints the command list and the key that opens them, and `/familytree menu` opens the chest menu.
- `vanillaMenu=false` in `config/familytree-server.properties` turns the menu off. `commandAliases=pets,ft` adds other names for `/familytree`.

The family tree is not drawn on a map item. Text on a 128-pixel map needs a server-side bitmap font, which would leave pet names in Chinese, Japanese, Korean, and other scripts unreadable, and each map adds a permanent file to the world.

## Translations

Family Tree now includes Simplified Chinese, Traditional Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Russian, Ukrainian, German, French, Italian, Spanish for Spain and for Mexico, Brazilian Portuguese, Polish, and Turkish. The translations are machine-made, and corrections are welcome on GitHub.

- The server also answers in these languages. Chat messages from commands and the linking stick, and all chest menu text, use the player's Minecraft language when a translation exists, and English otherwise. This works for players without the client mod too.
- Species names in the browser, picker, chat, and menus now come from Minecraft's own entity names, so they follow the game language. `/familytree list` and `/familytree info` no longer contain fixed English text.
- A unit test checks that every language file has exactly the keys and format arguments of the English file.

## Every animal you breed

Family Tree now records cows, mooshrooms, sheep, pigs, chickens, rabbits, goats, foxes, axolotls, frogs, bees, turtles, pandas, ocelots, hoglins, striders, sniffers, armadillos, and breedable animals from other mods, next to your pets.

- Wild animals stay out of the history until a player breeds them, uses a name tag on one, or clicks one with the linking stick. Breeding records the baby and both parents. The player who fed, named, or added them owns them in Family Tree, and babies of a recorded family stay with that family's owner.
- Families already in the history keep growing when their animals breed without a player.
- Frogs, sniffers, and turtles hatch from spawn or eggs, so Minecraft never links their babies to the parents. Breeding frogs or sniffers still adds both parents for the player; turtles need a name tag or the linking stick. Name or add the hatchlings, then use **Add parents**.
- Unnamed animals other than pets get their species and a short code as a name, such as "Sheep 3F2A1C". The name comes from the species id, so it stays the same when you change languages. The pet names stay free for pets.
- `/familytree info` and the linking stick work for every recorded animal. `/familytree list` stops after 100 lines on large farms.

## Variants and rare outcomes

Tree cards, the browser, the details panel, `/familytree info`, and exports show each animal's variant: axolotl, mooshroom, frog, and llama colors, wool color, panda main and hidden genes, and rabbit and fox types. Cows, pigs, and chickens show their warm, cold, or temperate variant on Minecraft 1.21.5 and newer.

- A gold ★ marks a blue axolotl, a brown mooshroom, or a brown panda, and exports explain the star in their legend.
- Select a baby to compare its variant with its parents'. The panel explains surprises: the parents' wool colors mixed, a panda shows a gene both parents carried hidden, a mooshroom changed color (a 1 in 1,024 chance), or a bred axolotl turned blue (about 1 in 1,200). Family Tree judges this at birth, so dyeing a sheep or a lightning strike later doesn't change the explanation.
- Search matches variant names, so "blue" finds blue axolotls.

## Portraits for every species

Every animal in the browser and tree shows its in-game model with its variant, including modded animals. Exports reuse those portraits: the screen briefly shows them on black and white while the image is prepared, and the PNG is still drawn in the background. Cats, wolves, and horses keep their texture portraits in exports.

## Browser

Once the history holds more than one kind of animal, the browser shows tabs for Pets, Farm, Wildlife, and Modded, and the species buttons follow the selected tab. A history with only pets looks as it did in 1.2.0. Species forests with many separate families wrap into rows instead of one long strip, and cards off the screen are no longer drawn.

## Server settings and storage limits

`config/familytree-server.properties` gains these settings. The [README](../../README.md#server-settings-for-animals) describes each one.

- `trackFarmAnimals`, `trackWildlife`, and `trackModdedAnimals` turn categories on or off. All three are on by default.
- `trackedSpecies` and `ignoredSpecies` override the categories for single species. Ignored species also stop new pets of that species.
- `farmRecordLimit`, 5,000 by default, caps unnamed animals other than pets. At the limit, breeding stops adding them and the breeder hears about it once. Pets and named animals are always recorded.
- `autoPruneFarmRecords` is off by default. When on, reaching the limit writes a JSON backup to `familytree-backups/`, then deletes the oldest deceased unnamed animals other than pets until three quarters of the limit remain. It never deletes pets, named animals, or living animals.
- `/familytree prune farm` deletes every deceased unnamed animal other than a pet after the same backup.

In a test with 5,000 unnamed farm animals, the history encoded for saving in 31 to 50 ms to a 364 KiB file, a full snapshot was 4 pages and 889 KiB, and the client received it in 116 to 196 ms in singleplayer. A cleanup backup at that size was 2.3 MB and took 45 to 91 ms.

## Fixes

- Deaths without an attacker now read like Minecraft's own death messages, such as "Rex suffocated in a wall on day 12". A pet that suffocated used to show "Died of in wall". The tree, the list command, the chest menu, and the book use the same wording in every language.
- Large families with 32 or more animals in one generation could stop the tree screen from opening because rows were sorted by positions that changed during the sort.

## Upgrade

Update the server and clients together. The snapshot and rename result channels changed to `snapshot_pages_v5` and `rename_result_v3`, and NeoForge and Forge register protocol 4. The chest menus and translations add no saved fields and no network channels.

Saved records gain one optional `variant_origin` field, which records how a bred animal got its variant. Variants themselves use the existing `variant_id` field. A 1.2.0 history loads unchanged. If you go back to 1.2.0, it keeps every record, including the new animals, and drops `variant_origin` on its next save.
